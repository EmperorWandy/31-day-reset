## What this tells us

1. **Your strengths are rare.** Only 2 of the 20 even mention offline use, and only 4 mention
   reminders. On-time reminders, offline use, a Nigerian voice coach and a fixed progressive plan are
   real selling points. Lead with them in the store listing.
2. **Syncing is expected.** 13 of the 20 connect to watches or other health apps. Health Connect
   in 7.2 closes this gap without adding a server.
3. **Food logging is mainstream.** Half the apps have it, but most count calories. A Nigerian food
   list with a simple plate check is different from anything in the top 20.
4. **Walking rewards rule the free chart.** WeWard, Sweatcoin, FitCash, aHealth and two step
   counters all rank high. You already count steps, so a daily step goal and step badges would be a
   cheap win.
5. **Social features are the biggest remaining gap** (16 of 20). Your WhatsApp buddy is a start.

## Ideas for next versions, in order

1. **Step goal and step badges:** a daily target (for example 7,000 steps) with a streak and badges.
   Small job, uses the step counter you already have.
2. **WhatsApp group challenge:** a weekly card a group can share ("Week 2: 5 of 7 days done") with a
   leaderboard built from what friends paste back. No server needed.
3. **Say more in the store listing:** most leaders list rest timers, offline use and reminders up
   front. Your app has all three, so add them to the Play description and screenshots.
4. **Pidgin or Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa coaching:** a strong local difference, but it changes the fixed
   Ezinne and Abeo decision, so it needs your approval.
5. **Wear OS tile:** Hevy, Gravl and Peloton offer watch apps. Large job; leave until after launch.

## Fixed decisions kept

Leaders let users build their own workouts (Lyfta, Hevy, Fitbod) or let AI change the plan (Runna,
Gravl, Google Health). 31-Day Reset keeps its fixed, progressive whole-body plans, as decided. The
food log gives general guidance only, with no calorie targets.
